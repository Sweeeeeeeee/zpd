Take a segment AB (free). Let U be the point with line AU at directed angle 60 degrees from line AB and line BU at 120 degrees from line BA, i.e. the apex of an equilateral triangle ABU on the left of A->B (counterclockwise convention). Let Z be any point on the line through A perpendicular to AU. Let C be the point on line AU such that CZ is perpendicular to ZU (so Z sees CU at a right angle with A the foot of the altitude: AC·AU = AZ^2, A between C and U). Let O be the circumcentre of triangle ABC, J its incentre. M2 = second intersection (other than A) of line JA with the circumcircle (centre O through A). M = reflection of M2 through O (antipode). Let P be the intersection of the tangents to the circumcircle at A and at B, and Q the intersection of the tangents at A and at C. In triangle OPQ let H be the orthocentre and I the incentre. N = midpoint of OI. D = second intersection (other than M) of line NM with the circle centre O through M. Prove that AD is perpendicular to HI.
