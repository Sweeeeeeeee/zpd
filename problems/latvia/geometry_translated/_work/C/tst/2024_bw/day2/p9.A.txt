Let ABC be an acute triangle. Let D be the foot of the altitude from A to BC. Z = reflection of C over line AB. W = circumcentre of triangle ABZ (circle through A, B, Z). V = any point on the circle with diameter AB; T = foot of the perpendicular from V to AB (so T is an arbitrary point of segment AB). X = the second intersection (other than Z) of line TZ with circle (W) through Z. X2 = reflection of X through point A (X2 = 2A - X).
S1 = X + B - D. J1 = incentre of triangle X X2 S1. P = reflection of S1 over line X J1. S2 = P + C - D. J2 = incentre of triangle P A S2. Q = reflection of S2 over line P J2.
Z2 = reflection of B over line AC. W2 = circumcentre of triangle A C Z2. W3 = W2 + C - A. Q3 = Q + C - A. Y5 = a common point of circle (centre W3 through C) and circle (centre C through Q3) (two intersection points possible, one of them presumably C-side; the construction does not say which). K3 = foot of perpendicular from Y5 to line C W3. K = K3 + A - C. F2 = foot of perpendicular from C to line A W2. Y1 = K + C - F2. P5 = the point with K3P5 perpendicular to K3Y5 and K3P5 = K3Y5, obtained by rotating Y5 about K3 by +90 degrees (counterclockwise assumed, the "psquare" convention). S6 = K + P5 - K3. J7 = incentre of triangle K Y1 S6. Y = reflection of S6 over line K J7.
X3 = reflection of X through point B (X3 = 2B - X). S3 = X + A - C. J4 = incentre of triangle X X3 S3. M = reflection of S3 over line X J4. Y3 = reflection of Y through point C (Y3 = 2C - Y). S4 = Y + A - B. J5 = incentre of triangle Y Y3 S4. N = reflection of S4 over line Y J5.
Prove that AM = AN.
