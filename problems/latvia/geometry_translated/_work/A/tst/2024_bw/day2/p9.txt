# dof: 3   (acute triangle: 2; X on its arc: 1; Y on its arc: 1; CD + AY = BD + AX: -1; M, N fixed)
# notes: Every point is built deterministically (no branch). Arcs: the reflection Z of C over AB lies on the circle of X (reflection of the circumcircle: inscribed angle 180°−C on C's side), likewise Z2 = reflection of B over AC on the circle Γ of Y. X = second intersection of line ZT with its circle, T the foot on AB of a free point V of the circle with diameter AB (T inside segment AB, so X is on C's side). 'Point on a ray at a given distance' (P, Q, M, N, Y): lay off S at that distance from the ray's origin K with shift/psquare, then reflect S over the internal bisector KJ of the angle between the ray and KS (J the incentre). P on ray AX beyond X with XP = BD; Q on ray PA with PQ = CD, so AQ = AX + BD − CD, and the hypothesis is AY = AQ. Y is the point of Γ with AY = AQ on C's side of the diameter AW2 of Γ (the minor arc AC on B's side lies in that half). To pick it without a branch: Y5 = one of the two intersections of the copy of Γ and of circle (A, AQ) translated by C − A (so no named point coincides with Y); K3 its foot on the copied diameter; K = K3 − (C − A) is the foot of Y on AW2 and h = K3Y5 the half-chord; Y is laid off from K at distance h on the ray parallel to F2→C (F2 the foot of C on AW2). M on ray BX beyond X with XM = AC, N on ray CY beyond Y with YN = AB. 'X, Y inside the triangle' is not imposed. Many drawings fail to build because the hypotheses have no solution there (AQ must be shorter than AC) or helper points nearly coincide.
